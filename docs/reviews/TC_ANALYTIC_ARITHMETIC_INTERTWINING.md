## 2. First-Principles Derivation of Both Grade Actions

### 2.1 Analytic TC Dilation ($A_K$)
For $K \in \mathbb A_{\mathbb R}$ and an arbitrary Dirichlet series $F(s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n n^{-s}$:
$$A_K[F](s) = F(\tau^{-K} s).$$
In the half-plane of absolute convergence:
$$A_K[F](s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n n^{-\tau^{-K} s} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n \left(n^{\tau^{-K}}\right)^{-s}.$$
Thus, analytic scaling of the complex variable $s \mapsto \tau^{-K} s$ induces an exponential transformation on the Dirichlet bases:
$$\boxed{n \mapsto n^{\tau^{-K}}.}$$
In logarithmic coordinates $x = \log n$:
$$x' = \log\left(n^{\tau^{-K}}\right) = \tau^{-K} \log n = \tau^{-K} x.$$
Therefore, **Analytic TC Dilation acts as pure Dilation in log-space**:
$$\boxed{T_K: x \mapsto \tau^{-K} x.}$$

### 2.2 Arithmetic-Grid Dilation ($D_K$)
The literal arithmetic grid of Transcendental Continuation is:
$$L_K = \tau^K (\mathbb Z \setminus \{0\}) = \{n\tau^K : n \in \mathbb Z \setminus \{0\}\}.$$
If one scales the integer bases $n \mapsto \tau^K n$ in the native Dirichlet series:
$$D_K[F](s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n (\tau^K n)^{-s} = \tau^{-K s} \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n n^{-s} = \tau^{-K s} F(s).$$
In logarithmic coordinates $x = \log n$:
$$x' = \log(\tau^K n) = \log n + K \log \tau = x + K \log \tau.$$
Therefore, **Arithmetic-Grid Dilation acts as pure Translation in log-space**:
$$\boxed{S_K: x \mapsto x + K \log \tau.}$$

Both operations are authentic, but they live in dual geometric categories: one is a **scale automorphism of the multiplicative group $\mathbb R_{>0}$**, while the other is an **isometry of the additive metric space $\mathbb R$**.

## 4. Operator Algebra and the Affine Commutator

### 4.1 Group Laws
Both families form 1-parameter abelian groups under addition in the grade index:
- $A_{K_1} A_{K_2} = A_{K_1 + K_2}, \quad A_0 = \mathrm{Id}, \quad A_K^{-1} = A_{-K}$
- $D_{J_1} D_{J_2} = D_{J_1 + J_2}, \quad D_0 = \mathrm{Id}, \quad D_J^{-1} = D_{-J}$

### 4.2 Non-Commutation and the Semidirect Relation
Evaluating $A_K D_J$ and $D_J A_K$ on an arbitrary function $F(s)$:
$$A_K(D_J[F])(s) = A_K[\tau^{-J s} F(s)] = \tau^{-J(\tau^{-K} s)} F(\tau^{-K} s) = \tau^{-J \tau^{-K} s} F(\tau^{-K} s),$$
$$D_J(A_K[F])(s) = D_J[F(\tau^{-K} s)] = \tau^{-J s} F(\tau^{-K} s).$$
They do **not** commute for $J \ne 0, K \ne 0$. Their ratio is:
$$\boxed{\frac{A_K D_J [F](s)}{D_J A_K [F](s)} = \tau^{J(1 - \tau^{-K}) s} = e^{J(1 - \tau^{-K}) s \log \tau}.}$$

Notice that:
$$\tau^{-J \tau^{-K} s} F(\tau^{-K} s) = D_{J \tau^{-K}}[A_K[F]](s).$$
Therefore, we obtain the **exact intertwining relation**:
$$\boxed{A_K D_J = D_{J \tau^{-K}} A_K.}$$
Or in conjugation form:
$$\boxed{A_K D_J A_K^{-1} = D_{J \tau^{-K}}.}$$
The group $G = \langle A_K, D_J \rangle$ is a **semidirect product**:
$$G = \mathbb R \rtimes \tau^{\mathbb A_{\mathbb R}} \cong \operatorname{Aff}_+(\mathbb R),$$
where dilation $A_K$ acts on the normal subgroup of multipliers $\{D_J\}$ by scaling the generator $J \mapsto J \tau^{-K}$.

### 4.3 Log-Space Commutator Discrepancy
In log-space $x = \log n$:
$$S_K T_J(x) = \tau^{-J} x + K \log \tau,$$
$$T_J S_K(x) = \tau^{-J} x + \tau^{-J} K \log \tau.$$
The affine commutator discrepancy is:
$$\boxed{S_K T_J(x) - T_J S_K(x) = (1 - \tau^{-J}) K \log \tau.}$$
This discrepancy is:
- Identically zero if $K = 0$ or $J = 0$;
- Nonzero for all $K \ne 0, J \ne 0$.

## 5. Comparison on Logarithmic Derivatives

For the arithmetic of prime powers, the logarithmic derivative $-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^s}$ is the fundamental generating series.

### 5.1 Analytic Action on $-\zeta'/\zeta$
Applying $s \mapsto \tau^{-K} s$:
$$-\frac{d}{ds} \log \zeta(\tau^{-K} s) = \tau^{-K} \left(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\tau^{-K} s)\right) = \tau^{-K} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Lambda(n)}{(n^{\tau^{-K}})^s}.$$
The arithmetic measure in log coordinates ($x = \log n$) is:
$$\mu_K^{\mathrm{analytic}} = \tau^{-K} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \Lambda(n) \delta_{\tau^{-K} \log n}.$$
The prime stations $\log n$ are **dilated** by $\tau^{-K}$, and the measure intensity is scaled by $\tau^{-K}$.

### 5.2 Arithmetic Grid Action on $-\zeta'/\zeta$
Applying $D_K[\zeta](s) = \tau^{-K s} \zeta(s)$:
$$-\frac{d}{ds} \log (\tau^{-K s} \zeta(s)) = -\frac{d}{ds} [-K s \log \tau + \log \zeta(s)] = K \log \tau - \frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s) = K \log \tau + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^s}.$$
If one instead translates the support of the prime measure:
$$\mu_K^{\mathrm{grid}} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \Lambda(n) \delta_{\log n + K \log \tau},$$
its Laplace/Dirichlet transform is:
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-s x} d\mu_K^{\mathrm{grid}}(x) = \tau^{-K s} \left(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)\right).$$

### 5.3 Contrast
Under analytic dilation, the spacing between prime stations shrinks (for $K > 0$) as $\tau^{-K} \log p$, while under grid dilation, the entire spectrum of prime stations is shifted rigidly by $K \log \tau$ without altering internal relative distances.

## 6. Euler Product Audit: Multiplicative Closure vs Additive Lattices

For $\Re(s) > 1$, $\zeta(s) = \prod_p (1 - p^{-s})^{-1}$.

### 6.1 Analytic Dilation Preserves the Euler Product
$$\zeta(\tau^{-K} s) = \prod_p \frac{1}{1 - p^{-\tau^{-K} s}} = \prod_p \frac{1}{1 - (p^{\tau^{-K}})^{-s}}.$$
Because $(p_1^{a_1} \cdots p_m^{a_m})^{\tau^{-K}} = (p_1^{\tau^{-K}})^{a_1} \cdots (p_m^{\tau^{-K}})^{a_m}$, every positive integer $n$ has a unique factorization into the generalized prime bases $\mathcal P_K = \{p^{\tau^{-K}} : p \text{ prime}\}$. The Euler product holds identically.

### 6.2 The Arithmetic Grid Cannot Carry an Euler Product
Can the grid $L_K = \tau^K (\mathbb Z \setminus \{0\})$ carry an intrinsic Euler product?
Suppose one formally defines "grid primes" as $\tau^K p$. The corresponding Euler product would be:
$$\prod_p \frac{1}{1 - (\tau^K p)^{-s}} = \prod_p \frac{1}{1 - \tau^{-K s} p^{-s}} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \tau^{-K \Omega(n) s} n^{-s},$$
where $\Omega(n)$ is the number of prime factors of $n$ with multiplicity.
- For $n = 1$, the coefficient is $1$.
- For $n = p$, the coefficient is $\tau^{-K s}$.
- For $n = p_1 p_2$, the coefficient is $\tau^{-2 K s}$.
- For $n = p^k$, the coefficient is $\tau^{-k K s}$.

This Dirichlet series is:
$$\boxed{\sum_{n=1}^\infty \tau^{-K \Omega(n) s} n^{-s} \ne \tau^{-K s} \zeta(s) \quad \text{and} \quad \ne \zeta(\tau^{-K} s).}$$
Furthermore, the product of two grid elements does not lie in $L_K$:
$$(\tau^K m) \cdot (\tau^K n) = \tau^{2K} m n \in L_{2K} \ne L_K \qquad (K \ne 0).$$
The grid $L_K$ is an additive lattice ($\tau^K \mathbb Z$), **not a multiplicative semigroup or ring**. It cannot generate primes by factorization.

## 7. Mellin Transform Duality and the Commuting Diagram

Let $\mathcal M[\phi](s) = \int_0^\infty \phi(x) x^{s-1} dx$ be the Mellin transform.

### 7.1 Linear Scaling in Position Space
Define linear scaling $M_a[\phi](x) = \phi(a x)$ for $a > 0$:
$$\mathcal M[M_a \phi](s) = \int_0^\infty \phi(a x) x^{s-1} dx = a^{-s} \int_0^\infty \phi(u) u^{s-1} du = a^{-s} \mathcal M[\phi](s).$$
Setting $a = \tau^K$:
$$\boxed{\mathcal M \circ M_{\tau^K} = D_K \circ \mathcal M.}$$
**Arithmetic-grid dilation $D_K$ is the exact Mellin image of linear scaling in $x$-space.**

### 7.2 Power Scaling in Position Space
Define power scaling $P_K[\phi](x) = \tau^K \phi(x^{\tau^K})$:
$$\mathcal M[P_K \phi](s) = \int_0^\infty \tau^K \phi(x^{\tau^K}) x^{s-1} dx = \int_0^\infty \phi(u) u^{\tau^{-K} s - 1} du = \mathcal M[\phi](\tau^{-K} s).$$
$$\boxed{\mathcal M \circ P_K = A_K \circ \mathcal M.}$$
**Analytic TC dilation $A_K$ is the exact Mellin image of power scaling in $x$-space.**

### 7.3 Verdict on the Commuting Diagram (Case C)
In $x$-space:
$$(M_{\tau^J} P_K \phi)(x) = \tau^K \phi(\tau^{J \tau^K} x^{\tau^K}),$$
$$(P_K M_{\tau^J} \phi)(x) = \tau^K \phi(\tau^J x^{\tau^K}).$$
The arguments $\tau^{J \tau^K} x^{\tau^K}$ and $\tau^J x^{\tau^K}$ differ by the non-trivial factor $\tau^{J(\tau^K - 1)}$.
No linear coordinate change or universal intertwiner intertwines translation and dilation across all test functions:
$$\boxed{\textbf{Case C: No canonical intertwining diagram commutes universally for all functions.}}$$

## 8. Completed Function $\xi(s)$ and Symmetry Breakage

For the completed function $\xi(s) = \frac{1}{2} s(s-1) \pi^{-s/2} \Gamma(s/2) \zeta(s)$, which satisfies $\xi(s) = \xi(1-s)$:

### 8.1 Analytic Dilation Preserves Reflection Symmetry
$$A_K[\xi](s) = \xi(\tau^{-K} s).$$
Reflecting about the shifted critical axis $\Re(s) = \tau^K / 2$ ($s \mapsto \tau^K - s$):
$$A_K[\xi](\tau^K - s) = \xi(\tau^{-K}(\tau^K - s)) = \xi(1 - \tau^{-K} s) = \xi(\tau^{-K} s) = A_K[\xi](s).$$
Analytic dilation **preserves the exact reflection functional equation**, shifting the critical axis from $\Re(s) = 1/2$ to $\Re(s) = \tau^K / 2$.

### 8.2 Arithmetic Grid Dilation Breaks Reflection Symmetry
$$D_K[\xi](s) = \tau^{-K s} \xi(s).$$
Evaluating at $1-s$:
$$D_K[\xi](1-s) = \tau^{-K(1-s)} \xi(1-s) = \tau^{-K + K s} \xi(s) = \tau^{-K + 2 K s} (\tau^{-K s} \xi(s)) = \tau^{K(2s - 1)} D_K[\xi](s).$$
Arithmetic dilation **breaks reflection symmetry** by the exponential automorphic factor:
$$\alpha_K(s) = \tau^{K(2s - 1)}.$$
For $s = 1/2 + \delta + i\gamma$:
$$2s - 1 = 2\delta + 2i\gamma \implies \alpha_K(s) = \tau^{2 K \delta} e^{2 i K \gamma \log \tau}.$$
Its modulus is:
$$\boxed{|\alpha_K(s)| = \tau^{2 K \delta}.}$$
- On the critical line ($\delta = 0$), $|\alpha_K| = 1$ is a pure phase.
- Off the critical line ($\delta \ne 0$), $|\alpha_K| = \tau^{2 K \delta} \ne 1$ exhibits exponential radial deviation!

## 9. Derivation of the Spectral Detector $B_\rho$ from the Commutator

This section derives the exact mathematical origin of the finite-grade reflection defect $B_\rho$.

### 9.1 Evaluation of the Commutator at the Zero Locus
Let $\zeta(\rho) = 0$ with $\rho = 1/2 + \delta + i\gamma$.
Under $A_K$, the zero moves to $s_0 = \tau^K \rho$.
Both $A_K D_J[\zeta]$ and $D_J A_K[\zeta]$ vanish at $s_0 = \tau^K \rho$.
Evaluating the respective multipliers at this zero locus:
1. Multiplier of $A_K D_J[\zeta]$:
   $$\tau^{-J \tau^{-K} s_0} = \tau^{-J \tau^{-K} (\tau^K \rho)} = \tau^{-J \rho}.$$
2. Multiplier of $D_J A_K[\zeta]$:
   $$\tau^{-J s_0} = \tau^{-J (\tau^K \rho)} = \tau^{-J \tau^K \rho}.$$
3. Multiplier Ratio:
   $$R(K, J; \rho) = \frac{\tau^{-J \rho}}{\tau^{-J \tau^K \rho}} = \tau^{J(\tau^K - 1)\rho}.$$
4. Normalization by Critical-Line Value ($\rho_0 = 1/2 + i\gamma$):
   $$R_0(K, J; \gamma) = \tau^{J(\tau^K - 1)(1/2 + i\gamma)}.$$
   $$R_{\mathrm{norm}}(K, J; \rho) = \frac{R(K, J; \rho)}{R_0(K, J; \gamma)} = \tau^{J(\tau^K - 1)\delta}.$$
5. Reflection Pairing ($\rho^\# = 1/2 - \delta + i\gamma$):
   $$R_{\mathrm{norm}}(K, J; \rho^\#) = \tau^{-J(\tau^K - 1)\delta}.$$

### 9.2 The Commutator Reflection Defect
Taking the reflection defect of the normalized commutator ratio:
$$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{comm}}(K, J; \rho) = |R_{\mathrm{norm}}(\rho)| + |R_{\mathrm{norm}}(\rho^\#)| - 2 = \tau^{J(\tau^K - 1)\delta} + \tau^{-J(\tau^K - 1)\delta} - 2.$$
Setting:
$$\boxed{K_{\mathrm{eff}} = J(\tau^K - 1),}$$
we obtain the exact identity:
$$\boxed{\mathcal D_{\mathrm{comm}}(K, J; \rho) = 4\sinh^2\left(\frac{K_{\mathrm{eff}}\delta\log\tau}{2}\right) = B_\rho(K_{\mathrm{eff}}).}$$

### 9.3 Bilateral Symmetrization
Alternatively, consider the arithmetic multiplier on the centered coordinate $z = \rho - 1/2 = \delta + i\gamma$:
$$m_K(z) = \tau^{-K z} \implies |m_K(z)| = \tau^{-K\delta}.$$
Bilateral symmetrization $K \leftrightarrow -K$:
$$|m_K(z)| + |m_{-K}(z)| - 2 = \tau^{-K\delta} + \tau^{K\delta} - 2 = 4\sinh^2\left(\frac{K\delta\log\tau}{2}\right) = B_\rho(K).$$

## 10. Answers to the 12 Deliverable Questions

### A. What are the two grade actions?
- Analytic TC Dilation: $A_K[F](s) = F(\tau^{-K} s)$.
- Arithmetic-Grid Dilation: $D_K[F](s) = \tau^{-K s} F(s)$.

### B. What arithmetic transformation does analytic scaling actually induce?
It induces an exponential power scaling on the Dirichlet bases:
$$n \mapsto n^{\tau^{-K}}.$$

### C. What transform does literal grid scaling induce?
It multiplies the Dirichlet series by an exponential factor:
$$F(s) \mapsto \tau^{-K s} F(s).$$

### D. Do $A_K$ and $D_J$ commute?
No. They satisfy the exact semidirect relation:
$$A_K D_J = D_{J \tau^{-K}} A_K,$$
with discrepancy ratio $\tau^{J(1 - \tau^{-K}) s}$.

### E. What is the log-space structure?
In logarithmic coordinates $x = \log n$:
- Arithmetic grid dilation is **translation**: $S_K(x) = x + K \log \tau$.
- Analytic TC dilation is **dilation**: $T_J(x) = \tau^{-J} x$.
- Commutator discrepancy: $S_K T_J(x) - T_J S_K(x) = (1 - \tau^{-J}) K \log \tau$.

### F. Is there a natural intertwiner?
In Mellin space, linear scaling in position space maps to $D_K$, and power scaling maps to $A_K$. No universal linear operator intertwines them across all test functions (**Case C**).

### G. What is generic Dirichlet-series algebra?
The semidirect relation $A_K D_J = D_{J \tau^{-K}} A_K$, the log-space affine commutator, and the Mellin duality hold for **every** Dirichlet series $\sum a_n n^{-s}$.

### H. Where does the Euler product first matter?
The Euler product requires multiplicativity. Analytic dilation preserves it ($(m n)^{\tau^{-K}} = m^{\tau^{-K}} n^{\tau^{-K}}$); arithmetic grid dilation breaks it because $\tau^K m \cdot \tau^K n = \tau^{2K} m n \notin L_K$. The grid $L_K$ cannot carry an Euler product.

### I. Does completion to $\xi$ improve the relation?
Analytic dilation preserves reflection symmetry $\xi(\tau^{-K} s) = \xi(1 - \tau^{-K} s)$ about $\Re(s) = \tau^K / 2$. Arithmetic dilation breaks reflection symmetry by the factor $\tau^{K(2s - 1)}$, whose modulus is $\tau^{2 K \delta}$.

### J. Does bilateral symmetrization produce the TC defect?
Yes. Bilateral symmetrization of the arithmetic multiplier modulus on the centered zero ray yields:
$$|\tau^{-K(\rho - 1/2)}| + |\tau^{K(\rho - 1/2)}| - 2 = \tau^{K\delta} + \tau^{-K\delta} - 2 = B_\rho(K).$$

### K. Does any resulting functional have spectral expansion $\sum_\rho w_\rho B_\rho(K)$?
Evaluating the affine commutator ratio at the zeros yields $B_\rho(K_{\mathrm{eff}})$ pointwise at every zero. However, summing these pointwise evaluations into a global, zero-independent arithmetic functional $\mathscr A_K$ requires regularized explicit formula integration.

### L. What is the earliest remaining implication?
$$\boxed{\int_0^\infty \psi_K(x) d\left(\mu^{\mathrm{analytic}}_K - \mu^{\mathrm{grid}}_K\right)(x) = \sum_{\rho \in Z^+} w_\rho B_\rho(K) \quad \text{with } w_\rho > 0.}$$
