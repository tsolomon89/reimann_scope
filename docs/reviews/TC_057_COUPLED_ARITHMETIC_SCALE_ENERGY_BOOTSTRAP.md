### Proposition 5.2 (Linear vs. Quadratic Cutoff Replacement — Three-Quantity Separation, `TC-057R` §3B)

For $\Delta_p(X) := Q_{\lambda, L_p}(X/p) - Q_{\lambda, T(Y/p)}(X/p)$ across primes $U \le p \le Y$, define separately:
1. **Actual signed aggregate cutoff error**: $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{cut,lin}}^{\mathrm{signed}}(Y) := \int_Y^{\tau Y} \left(\sum_{U \le p \le Y} p^{-1/2} \Delta_p(X)\right)^2 \frac{dX}{X}$;
2. **Squared Minkowski sum of individual error norms**: $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{cut,lin}}^{\mathrm{Mink}}(Y) := \left(\sum_{U \le p \le Y} p^{-1/2} \|\Delta_p\|_{L^2([Y,\tau Y], dX/X)}\right)^2$;
3. **Squared unsigned analytic majorant** (from $B_p := \sqrt{\frac{\tau^5 - 1}{20}\max(16/p, 1)}$ in Theorem 5.1(2)): $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{cut,lin}}^{\mathrm{maj}}(Y) := \left(\sum_{U \le p \le Y} p^{-1/2} B_p\right)^2$.

*Proved bounds*: By Minkowski's integral inequality and $\|\Delta_p\|_{L^2} \le B_p$,
$$0 \le \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{cut,lin}}^{\mathrm{signed}}(Y) \le \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{cut,lin}}^{\mathrm{Mink}}(Y) \le \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{cut,lin}}^{\mathrm{maj}}(Y), \qquad \text{with} \quad \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{cut,lin}}^{\mathrm{maj}}(Y) \asymp \frac{Y}{(\log Y)^2}$$
for polylogarithmic $U(Y)$ (since $B_p = \sqrt{(\tau^5-1)/20}$ is a nonzero constant for $p \ge 16$ and $\sum_{U \le p \le Y} p^{-1/2} \asymp \frac{\sqrt{Y}}{\log Y}$). ## 6. Route D: Unconditional Diagonal Contraction and Proved Conditional Same-Scale Absorption Transfer

Dividing $(\log X) Q_{\lambda,T}(X) = \Sigma_{\lambda,\mathrm{pp}}(X; T) + Q_{\lambda,T}^{[1]}(X)$ by $\log X > 0$ ($Y \ge 2$) and expanding $(\Sigma_{\lambda,\mathrm{pp}}(X; T)/\log X)^2$ directly without Cauchy–Schwarz preserves the **exact outer factor $2$** ([`quadratic_prime_power_diagonal_off_diagonal_decomposition_tc057`](file:///c:/Development/Projects/reimann_scope/formal/RiemannScope/LiouvilleSquareDilation.lean), Lean Theorem 24):

where $E_{\mathrm{sh},1}(Y) := \int_Y^{\tau Y} \left(\frac{Q_{\lambda,T(Y)}^{[1]}(X)}{\log X}\right)^2 \frac{dX}{X}$,
$$\widetilde{D}_{\lambda,\mathrm{pp}}(Y) := \sum_{p^k \le T(Y)} \frac{(\log p)^2}{p^k} \int_Y^{\tau Y} Q_{\lambda,L_{p^k}}\!\left(\frac{X}{p^k}\right)^2 \frac{dX}{X (\log X)^2}, \quad \widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\lambda,\mathrm{pp}}(Y) := \sum_{\substack{p_1^{k_1},\, p_2^{k_2} \le T(Y) \\ p_1^{k_1} \neq p_2^{k_2}}} \frac{(-1)^{k_1 + k_2} (\log p_1)(\log p_2)}{p_1^{k_1/2} p_2^{k_2/2}} \int_Y^{\tau Y} Q_{\lambda,L_{p_1^{k_1}}}\!\left(\frac{X}{p_1^{k_1}}\right) Q_{\lambda,L_{p_2^{k_2}}}\!\left(\frac{X}{p_2^{k_2}}\right) \frac{dX}{X (\log X)^2}.$$

### Theorem 6.2 (Proved Route D Same-Scale Absorption Transfer — `CONDITIONAL_ON_SIGNED_CORRELATION`, Lean Theorem 26)

*Scope Label*: **`CONDITIONAL_ON_SIGNED_CORRELATION`**. Suppose the **open** signed-correlation hypothesis holds: for every $\eta > 0$ there exist $A_\eta \ge 0$, $\theta_\eta \in [0, 1/2)$, and $Y_\eta \ge 2$ such that
$$\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\lambda,\mathrm{pp}}(Y) + E_{\mathrm{sh},1}(Y) \le A_\eta Y^\eta + \theta_\eta F(Y) \qquad \text{for all } Y \ge Y_\eta.$$
Then **for every $\varepsilon > 0$**, there exists $M_\varepsilon \ge 0$ such that $E(Y) \le F(Y) \le M_\varepsilon Y^\varepsilon$ uniformly for all $Y \ge 1$.

*Proof* (without continuity of $E$, attainment of suprema, or a priori global finiteness of $\sup_{Z \ge 1} E(Z)/Z^\varepsilon$; machine-checked in [`conditional_signed_correlation_same_scale_absorption_transfer_tc057r`](file:///c:/Development/Projects/reimann_scope/formal/RiemannScope/LiouvilleSquareDilation.lean), Lean Theorem 26):
1. Fix $\varepsilon > 0$ and choose $\eta \in (0, \varepsilon)$ (e.g., $\eta = \varepsilon / 2$). For every finite horizon $R \ge 1$, define
   $$G_\varepsilon(R) := \sup_{1 \le Z \le R} \frac{E(Z)}{Z^\varepsilon}.$$
   By Proposition 2.1, $0 \le E(Z) \le M_{\mathrm{lin}} Z \le M_{\mathrm{lin}} R$ and $Z^\varepsilon \ge 1$ on $Z \in [1, R]$, so $0 \le \frac{E(Z)}{Z^\varepsilon} \le M_{\mathrm{lin}} R$. Hence $G_\varepsilon(R) \in [0, M_{\mathrm{lin}} R]$ is finite for every finite $R \ge 1$, and for every $W \in [1, R]$ and every $Z \in [1, W]$, $E(Z) = \frac{E(Z)}{Z^\varepsilon} Z^\varepsilon \le G_\varepsilon(R) W^\varepsilon$, whence taking the supremum over $Z \in [1, W]$ gives $F(W) \le G_\varepsilon(R) W^\varepsilon$.
2. Because $\theta_\eta \in [0, 1/2)$, $2\theta_\eta < 1$. Choose $q \in (2\theta_\eta, 1)$ (e.g., $q = \theta_\eta + 1/2$). By Theorem 6.1(2), $2\Theta_\varepsilon^{\mathrm{diag}}(Y) < \frac{4\zeta''(1+\varepsilon)}{(\log Y)^2} \to 0$ as $Y \to \infty$, so there exists $Y_* \ge \max(2, Y_\eta)$ such that
   $$2\theta_\eta + 2\Theta_\varepsilon^{\mathrm{diag}}(Y) \le q < 1 \qquad \text{for all } Y \ge Y_*.$$
   (Notice why $\theta_\eta < 1/2$ is sharp: if $\theta \ge 1/2$, then $2\theta + 2\Theta_\varepsilon^{\mathrm{diag}}(Y) \ge 1$ because the outer inequality has $2[\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\lambda,\mathrm{pp}}(Y) + E_{\mathrm{sh},1}(Y)]$.)
3. Let $R \ge Y_*$ and let $Y \in [Y_*, R]$. For every prime power $p^k \le Y$, $W := Y / p^k \in [1, Y] \subseteq [1, R]$, so $F(Y/p^k) \le G_\varepsilon(R) (Y/p^k)^\varepsilon$; similarly $F(Y) \le G_\varepsilon(R) Y^\varepsilon$. Combining $E(Y) \le 2\widetilde{D}_{\lambda,\mathrm{pp}}(Y) + 2[\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\lambda,\mathrm{pp}}(Y) + E_{\mathrm{sh},1}(Y)]$ with Theorem 6.1(1) and the assumed bound on $\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\lambda,\mathrm{pp}}(Y) + E_{\mathrm{sh},1}(Y)$ gives
   $$E(Y) \le 2\Theta_\varepsilon^{\mathrm{diag}}(Y) G_\varepsilon(R) Y^\varepsilon + 2 C_{\mathrm{cut,diag}} + 2 A_\eta Y^\eta + 2\theta_\eta G_\varepsilon(R) Y^\varepsilon.$$
   Dividing by $Y^\varepsilon > 0$ and noting $Y \ge Y_*$ with $\eta - \varepsilon < 0$ and $-\varepsilon < 0$ yields $Y^{\eta - \varepsilon} \le Y_*^{\eta - \varepsilon}$ and $Y^{-\varepsilon} \le Y_*^{-\varepsilon}$, so
   $$\frac{E(Y)}{Y^\varepsilon} \le K + \bigl(2\theta_\eta + 2\Theta_\varepsilon^{\mathrm{diag}}(Y)\bigr) G_\varepsilon(R) \le K + q G_\varepsilon(R), \qquad K := 2 A_\eta Y_*^{\eta - \varepsilon} + 2 C_{\mathrm{cut,diag}} Y_*^{-\varepsilon},$$
   where $K$ is **independent of $R$**.
4. For $Z \in [1, Y_*]$, $\frac{E(Z)}{Z^\varepsilon} \le G_\varepsilon(Y_*)$; for $Z \in [Y_*, R]$, step (3) gives $\frac{E(Z)}{Z^\varepsilon} \le K + q G_\varepsilon(R)$. Taking the supremum over $Z \in [1, R]$ yields
   $$G_\varepsilon(R) \le \max\!\bigl(G_\varepsilon(Y_*),\; K + q G_\varepsilon(R)\bigr).$$
   Because $G_\varepsilon(R) < \infty$ and $1 - q > 0$, if $G_\varepsilon(R) \le K + q G_\varepsilon(R)$ then $(1 - q) G_\varepsilon(R) \le K$, i.e., $G_\varepsilon(R) \le \frac{K}{1 - q}$. In both cases,
   $$G_\varepsilon(R) \le M_\varepsilon := \max\!\left(G_\varepsilon(Y_*),\; \frac{K}{1 - q}\right) \qquad \text{for every } R \ge Y_*.$$
5. Finally, for any $Y \ge 1$, set $R := \max(Y, Y_*) \ge Y_*$. Since $Y \in [1, R]$, step (1) and step (4) give $E(Y) \le F(Y) \le G_\varepsilon(R) Y^\varepsilon \le M_\varepsilon Y^\varepsilon$. $\square$
