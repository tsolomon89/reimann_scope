## 1. Executive Summary and Problem Statement

TASK-TC-021 demonstrated that the single-frequency Riemann Converter:
$$T_{\sigma, t}(x) = \Re\left(\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\mu(n)}{n} \operatorname{Ei}\left(\frac{(\sigma+it)\log x}{n}\right)\right)$$
exhibits exact scale covariance $T_{c\sigma, ct}(x^{1/c}) = T_{\sigma, t}(x)$ for any positive real scale factor $c > 0$. This confirmed that the survival of harmonic tuning is a general conformal scaling symmetry (`GENERIC_CONVERTER_SCALE_COVARIANCE`), while the prime station pushforward $u \mapsto \tau^{-K} u$ (mapping primes to $p^{\tau^{-K}}$) belongs specifically to the auxiliary analytic coordinate dilation $\zeta(\tau^{-K} s)$.

This sprint reconciles the repository's foundational arithmetic structure with the intended construction:
$$L_K = \tau^K \mathbb{Z}, \qquad P_K = \tau^K \mathbb{P}, \qquad K \in \mathbb{Z}, \qquad \tau = 2\pi.$$
Under this intended structure, the elements:
$$2\tau, \quad 2, \quad \frac{2}{\tau}$$
are three graded realizations of the **same prime referent $2$** under three different $\tau$-unit metrics.

The primary objective of this review is to determine the exact standard algebraic structure expressing this construction, prove its mathematical properties, audit its relation to the Riemann zeta function, and determine whether the faithful grading provides a cross-grade algebraic constraint forcing $\operatorname{Re}(\rho) = 1/2$.

## 2. Answers to Governing Questions A through P

### A. What exact algebraic object models $n\tau^K$?
The elements $n\tau^K$ (for $n \in \mathbb{Z}, K \in \mathbb{Z}$) are homogeneous elements of degree $K$ in the subring of $\mathbb{R}$ generated by $\mathbb{Z}$, $\tau$, and $\tau^{-1}$:
$$R_\tau = \mathbb{Z}[\tau, \tau^{-1}] \subset \mathbb{R}.$$
Each homogeneous element $x = n\tau^K$ has coefficient $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ and grade $K \in \mathbb{Z}$.

### B. Is it isomorphic to a Laurent polynomial ring?
**Yes.**  
Consider the formal Laurent polynomial ring in one indeterminate:
$$\mathbb{Z}[X, X^{-1}] = \left\{\sum_{K=-M}^N a_K X^K : M, N \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}, \; a_K \in \mathbb{Z}\right\}.$$
Define the evaluation homomorphism at $X = \tau$:
$$\operatorname{ev}_\tau : \mathbb{Z}[X, X^{-1}] \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad X \mapsto \tau.$$
- **Surjectivity**: The image of $\operatorname{ev}_\tau$ is by definition $R_\tau = \mathbb{Z}[\tau, \tau^{-1}]$.
- **Injectivity**: Suppose $P(X) = \sum_{K=-M}^N a_K X^K \in \ker(\operatorname{ev}_\tau)$. Then $\sum_{K=-M}^N a_K \tau^K = 0$. Multiplying by $\tau^M$ gives:
  $$\sum_{j=0}^{M+N} a_{j-M} \tau^j = 0.$$
  This is an ordinary polynomial with integer coefficients vanishing at $\tau = 2\pi$. By Lindemann's theorem (1882), $\pi$ is transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$, hence $\tau = 2\pi$ is transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$. Therefore every coefficient must vanish: $a_{j-M} = 0$ for all $j$, so $a_K = 0$ for all $K \in \{-M, \dots, N\}$.
  Thus $\ker(\operatorname{ev}_\tau) = \{0\}$.
- **Conclusion**: $\operatorname{ev}_\tau$ is an isomorphism of rings:
  $$\boxed{R_\tau \cong \mathbb{Z}[X, X^{-1}].}$$

### C. Why does transcendence make the grading faithful?
The formal ring $\mathbb{Z}[X, X^{-1}]$ possesses a canonical $\mathbb{Z}$-grading given by the power of $X$:
$$\mathbb{Z}[X, X^{-1}] = \bigoplus_{K \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z} X^K.$$
Because $\operatorname{ev}_\tau$ is an injective ring isomorphism, this grading transfers faithfully to $R_\tau$:
$$R_\tau = \bigoplus_{K \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z} \tau^K.$$
Faithfulness means that this sum is **strictly direct**:
$$\sum_{j=1}^r a_j \tau^{K_j} = 0 \quad (K_j \text{ distinct integers}, \; a_j \in \mathbb{Z}) \implies a_1 = \dots = a_r = 0.$$
If $\tau$ were algebraic (for example, $\tau = \sqrt{2}$), then $\tau^2 - 2\tau^0 = 0$, which would mix grade $2$ and grade $0$ non-trivially, destroying faithful grading. The transcendence of $\tau$ ensures that distinct integer powers $\tau^K$ never satisfy any non-trivial linear relation over $\mathbb{Z}$ (or over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$).

### D. What are its units?
In any Laurent polynomial ring $D[X, X^{-1}]$ over an integral domain $D$, the unit group is:
$$U(D[X, X^{-1}]) = \{u X^K : u \in U(D), \; K \in \mathbb{Z}\}.$$
Since $D = \mathbb{Z}$ and $U(\mathbb{Z}) = \{+1, -1\}$, we have:
$$\boxed{U(R_\tau) \cong U(\mathbb{Z}[X, X^{-1}]) = \{\pm \tau^K : K \in \mathbb{Z}\}.}$$
Thus the elements $\tau^K$ are precisely the canonical non-trivial units of $R_\tau$. Each integer grade $K$ corresponds to multiplication by the invertible unit $\tau^K$.

### E. In what exact sense is $p\tau^K$ prime?
Let $p \in \mathbb{P} = \{2, 3, 5, 7, 11, \dots\}$ be an ordinary rational prime.
In the ring $R_\tau \cong \mathbb{Z}[X, X^{-1}]$, consider the principal ideal $(p)$. The quotient ring is:
$$R_\tau / (p) \cong \mathbb{Z}[X, X^{-1}] / (p) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})[X, X^{-1}] = \mathbb{F}_p[X, X^{-1}].$$
Because $p$ is prime, $\mathbb{F}_p$ is a field. The polynomial ring $\mathbb{F}_p[X]$ is an integral domain (in fact a PID and UFD), and its localization by the powers of $X$, $\mathbb{F}_p[X, X^{-1}]$, is also an integral domain.
Because $R_\tau / (p)$ is an integral domain, $p$ is a **prime element** of $R_\tau$.
Furthermore, because $\tau^K \in U(R_\tau)$ is a unit, multiplying $p$ by $\tau^K$ produces an associate element:
$$p\tau^K = (\tau^K) p \sim p \quad \text{in } R_\tau.$$
In any commutative ring, multiplying a prime element by a unit preserves primality.
Therefore:
$$\boxed{p\tau^K \text{ is a prime element of } R_\tau \text{ associate to } p.}$$
It is not an ordinary rational integer; it is a graded prime element of the TC Laurent ring.

### F. Are the different $P_K$ distinct yet the same prime system?
**Yes.**  
For each grade $K \in \mathbb{Z}$, define:
$$P_K = \{p\tau^K : p \in \mathbb{P}\}.$$
- **Distinct as sets in $\mathbb{R}$**: For $K \ne J$, $P_K \cap P_J = \emptyset$, because $p\tau^K = q\tau^J$ implies $\tau^{J-K} = p/q \in \mathbb{Q}$, contradicting the transcendence of $\tau$.
- **Equivalent as prime associate classes**: For each prime referent $p \in \mathbb{P}$, the elements $p\tau^K \in P_K$ and $p\tau^J \in P_J$ generate the exact same principal ideal:
  $$(p\tau^K) = (p) = (p\tau^J) \subset R_\tau.$$
Thus $P_K$ and $P_J$ are distinct homogeneous realizations of the **same abstract prime system** under different grade units.

### G. Can lines intersect?
For $K \in \mathbb{Z}$, define the grade lines:
$$L_K = \{n\tau^K : n \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}\}, \qquad L_K^0 = \{n\tau^K : n \in \mathbb{Z}\} = \tau^K \mathbb{Z}.$$
- **Non-zero stations**: For $K \ne J$:
  $$\boxed{L_K \cap L_J = \emptyset.}$$
  Proof: If $n\tau^K = m\tau^J$ with $n, m \ne 0$, then $n\tau^K - m\tau^J = 0$, violating the linear independence of distinct powers of $\tau$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
- **Including zero**:
  $$L_K^0 \cap L_J^0 = \{0\}.$$
- **Over algebraic numbers**:
  $$\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\tau^K \cap \overline{\mathbb{Q}}\tau^J = \{0\} \qquad (K \ne J).$$

### H. Can an algebraic multiplier transfer one nonzero station between grades?
**No.**  
Let $x = m\tau^K \in L_K$ and $y = n\tau^J \in L_J$ with $m, n \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^\times$ and $K \ne J$.
Suppose there exists an algebraic scalar $\alpha \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^\times$ such that $\alpha x = y$.
Then:
$$\alpha (m\tau^K) = n\tau^J \implies \tau^{J-K} = \frac{\alpha m}{n} \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^\times.$$
Since $J - K \ne 0$ is an integer and $\tau = 2\pi$ is transcendental, $\tau^{J-K}$ is transcendental. But $\alpha m / n \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is algebraic.
Contradiction!  
Therefore, no algebraic scalar can transfer a non-zero station between distinct grades (`NO_ALGEBRAIC_TRANSFER_STATION`).

### I. Can finite algebraic linear combinations cancel between grades?
**No.**  
Let $K_1, \dots, K_r$ be distinct integers and $a_1, \dots, a_r \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$. If:
$$\sum_{j=1}^r a_j \tau^{K_j} = 0,$$
then multiplying by $\tau^M$ (where $M = \max(0, -K_1, \dots, -K_r)$) yields an algebraic polynomial in $\tau$ vanishing at $\tau$. By Lindemann's theorem, $\tau$ is transcendental over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.
Therefore:
$$\boxed{a_1 = \dots = a_r = 0.}$$
Distinct grade components can **never cancel linearly** over algebraic coefficients (`FINITE_CROSS_GRADE_LINEAR_INDEPENDENCE`).

### J. How can grades cancel multiplicatively?
While distinct grades cannot cancel additively, grades **add and can balance to zero under multiplication**:
$$(p\tau^K)(q\tau^{-K}) = pq \tau^0 = pq \in \mathbb{Z} = R_0.$$
More generally, for any homogeneous elements $x = a\tau^K \in R_K$ and $y = b\tau^J \in R_J$:
$$x y = (a\tau^K)(b\tau^J) = ab \tau^{K+J} \in R_{K+J}, \qquad \deg(xy) = \deg x + \deg y.$$
When $K + J = 0$, the product lies in $R_0 = \mathbb{Z}$.
Thus:
- **Additive rigidity**: Different grades cannot cancel linearly over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.
- **Multiplicative conservation**: Grades add algebraically under multiplication; opposite grades $\tau^K$ and $\tau^{-K}$ cancel to the identity $1$.
The broad claim "transcendence can never cancel" is false once opposite grades are multiplied.

### K. What is the grade-zero algebraicity selection rule?
Let $F(X) = \sum_{K=-M}^N a_K X^K \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}[X, X^{-1}]$ be a finite Laurent polynomial with algebraic coefficients.
Suppose its evaluated value at $\tau$ is algebraic:
$$F(\tau) = \alpha \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}.$$
Then:
$$(a_0 - \alpha)\tau^0 + \sum_{K \ne 0} a_K \tau^K = 0.$$
By the Finite Cross-Grade Linear Independence Theorem:
1. For all $K \ne 0$: $a_K = 0$.
2. For $K = 0$: $a_0 - \alpha = 0 \implies \alpha = a_0$.
Therefore:
$$\boxed{F(\tau) \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}} \iff a_K = 0 \quad \forall K \ne 0 \iff F(X) = a_0 \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}.}$$
A finite Laurent expression with algebraic coefficients evaluates to an algebraic native value if and only if **all nonzero-grade components vanish identically** (`GRADE_ZERO_ALGEBRAICITY_SELECTION`).

For a monomial product $M = a \prod_{j=1}^r (p_j \tau^{K_j})^{e_j}$ with $a \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^\times, e_j \in \mathbb{Z}$:
$$M = A \tau^G, \qquad A = a \prod_{j=1}^r p_j^{e_j} \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^\times, \qquad G = \sum_{j=1}^r e_j K_j.$$
$$M \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}} \iff G = 0 \quad (`TOTAL_GRADE_ZERO_ALGEBRAICITY`).$$

### L. What is the correct Dirichlet/zeta function of a grade line?
For the positive integer lattice $L_K^+ = \{n\tau^K : n \ge 1\}$, the direct Dirichlet series is:
$$Z_K^{\mathrm{grid}}(s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty (n\tau^K)^{-s} = \tau^{-Ks} \sum_{n=1}^\infty n^{-s} = \boxed{\tau^{-Ks} \zeta(s).}$$
Because $\tau^{-Ks} = \exp(-Ks\log\tau) \ne 0$ for all $s \in \mathbb{C}$, the zero set satisfies:
$$Z_K^{\mathrm{grid}}(s) = 0 \iff \zeta(s) = 0.$$
Every arithmetic grade has **the exact same zero set**. Zeros do **not** move under the arithmetic grid grading.

The Euler product is:
$$Z_K^{\mathrm{grid}}(s) = \tau^{-Ks} \prod_{p \in \mathbb{P}} (1 - p^{-s})^{-1}.$$
Notice that the Euler product factors the ordinary prime powers $p^{-s}$, with a single global prefactor $\tau^{-Ks}$.  
If one attempted to substitute $p\tau^K$ independently into each Euler factor:
$$\prod_{p \in \mathbb{P}} (1 - (p\tau^K)^{-s})^{-1} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \tau^{-K\Omega(n)s} n^{-s},$$
where $\Omega(n)$ is the total number of prime factors of $n$ with multiplicity. For composite $n$, $\Omega(n) > 1$, which would assign grade $K\Omega(n)$ to $n$, mixing different grades and destroying the single-grade lattice $L_K$. In $R_\tau$, composite numbers factor as $n\tau^K = \tau^K \prod_p p^{v_p(n)}$, with a single grade unit $\tau^K$.

### M. How does it differ from $\zeta(\tau^{-K} s)$?

### N. Which construction should be called canonical arithmetic TC?
$Z_K^{\mathrm{grid}}(s) = \tau^{-Ks}\zeta(s)$ is the **canonical arithmetic TC zeta family**, because it directly corresponds to the arithmetic grid $L_K = \tau^K \mathbb{Z}$ and the Laurent ring $R_\tau$.  
The function $Z_K^{\mathrm{pull}}(s) = \zeta(\tau^{-K}s)$ is reclassified as an **auxiliary analytic coordinate dilation**.

### O. Does any existing zeta identity produce the required finite mixed-grade algebraic relation?
**No.**  
We audited all natural candidates:
1. **Euler product ratios**: $Z_K^{\mathrm{grid}}(s) / Z_J^{\mathrm{grid}}(s) = \tau^{-(K-J)s}$. At a zero $\rho$, both evaluate to $0$, giving $0/0$.
2. **Logarithmic derivative**: $-\frac{d}{ds}\log Z_K^{\mathrm{grid}}(s) = K\log\tau - \frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)$. The coefficient $K\log\tau$ contains $\log\tau = \log(2\pi)$, which is transcendental, violating the algebraic coefficient firewall.
3. **Bilateral multiplication**: $(p\tau^K)(p\tau^{-K}) = p^2 \in \mathbb{Z}$. The total grade is zero ($K - K = 0$). On the spectral side, $|\eta_\rho(K)| \cdot |\eta_\rho(K)|^{-1} = \tau^{K\delta} \tau^{-K\delta} = 1$. The off-critical exponent cancels identically to zero without requiring $\delta = 0$.
4. **Bilateral addition**: The defect $B_\rho(K) = \tau^{K\delta} + \tau^{-K\delta} - 2$ is non-zero for $\delta \ne 0$, but no identity in zeta theory equates $B_\rho(K)$ to an algebraic number.
Therefore, current zeta identities do not produce a non-trivial finite mixed-grade algebraic relation (`NO_CURRENT_ZETA_CROSS_GRADE_BRIDGE`).

### P. What is the exact next bridge if none does?
The earliest missing bridge is:
> **An authentic arithmetic or spectral identity connecting the values of the Riemann zeta function or its explicit formula across distinct grades in a finite linear combination with proven algebraic coefficients.**

Without such an identity:
- Faithful Laurent grading is mathematically proved and structurally complete.
- Grade-zero selection is an exact algebraic theorem.
- But zeta arithmetic does not violate faithful grading off the critical line, because natural bilateral pairings balance total grade to zero identically.
