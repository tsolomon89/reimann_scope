## 2. Preserved Endpoint Contradiction, Premise Table, TC Scaling Distinction, and Window-Base Scope (`Section 1`)

### 2.1 Actual-`riemannZeta` Pole Contradiction, Reflection, Trivial Zeros, and Compensating Poles

On explicit real-part/imaginary-part coordinates $z = x + i y$ ($x, y \in \mathbb{R}$), Euler's product on $x > 1$ gives
$$L_\lambda(x + i y) := \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda(n)}{n^{x + i y}} = \frac{\zeta(2x + 2 i y)}{\zeta(x + i y)} \qquad (x > 1).$$
Preserving [`liouville_dirichlet_ratio_pole_contradiction_and_reflection_tc063`](../../formal/RiemannScope/DyadicChainCorrelationRegularity.lean#L390-L512) (`PROVED_IN_LEAN` on `Mathlib.riemannZeta` and `completedRiemannZeta`):

- **Right half-strip ($1/2 < x < 1$)**: Given an independently constructed pole-free holomorphic continuation $F_{\mathrm{hol}}(z)$ of $L_\lambda(z)$ to $\{z = x + i y : x > 1/2\}$, identity of analytic continuation on $\{x > 1/2\} \setminus \{1\}$ gives $\zeta(z) F_{\mathrm{hol}}(z) = \zeta(2z)$. At any hypothetical zero $\rho = x + i y$ with $1/2 < x < 1$, $\operatorname{Re}(2\rho) = 2x > 1$ lies in the Euler-product half-plane so $\zeta(2\rho) \ne 0$, forcing the immediate contradiction
  $$0 = \zeta(\rho)\,F_{\mathrm{hol}}(\rho) = \zeta(2\rho) \ne 0.$$
- **Left half-strip ($0 < x < 1/2$)**: Directly at $\rho = x + i y$ with $0 < x < 1/2$, $2\rho$ has $0 < 2x < 1$ inside the critical strip where $\zeta(2\rho) \ne 0$ is **not** known a priori; instead, `completedRiemannZeta` reflection $\Lambda_\zeta(1 - z) = \Lambda_\zeta(z)$ maps $\rho$ to the right-half-strip zero $1 - \rho = (1 - x) + i(-y)$ with $1/2 < 1 - x < 1$ and $\operatorname{Re}(2(1 - \rho)) = 2(1 - x) > 1$, where the nonremovable pole contradiction occurs.
- **Trivial zeros $-2(n + 1)$ ($n \ge 0$)**: At $z = -2(n + 1)$, both numerator $\zeta(-4(n + 1)) = 0$ and denominator $\zeta(-2(n + 1)) = 0$ vanish simultaneously, so no pole or contradiction arises on $x \le 0$.
- **Why meromorphic continuation alone proves nothing**: $\zeta(2z)/\zeta(z)$ is already unconditionally meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$; at a zero $\rho$ of $\zeta(z)$ with $\zeta(2\rho) \ne 0$, the meromorphic quotient simply carries a compensating pole so $\zeta(z)\cdot(\zeta(2z)/\zeta(z)) \to \zeta(2\rho)$ without contradiction. Only **pole-free holomorphy** of $F_{\mathrm{hol}}$ excludes $\zeta(\rho) = 0$.

### 2.2 Fixed Square Dilation, Power Lift, Liouville Convolution, TC Grade Transport, and Window-Base Scope

1. **Fixed Square Dilation vs. Running-Index Power Lift vs. Liouville Convolution vs. $\tau^K$ Grade Transport**: Four distinct operations enter the squarefree-core analysis and must not be conflated (in particular, fixed square dilation does **not** double the complex argument $x + i y$):
   - *Fixed square dilation* (for a fixed positive integer $b \ge 1$): multiplying summation indices by $b^2$ multiplies a Dirichlet series by the scalar prefactor $b^{-2(x + i y)}$,
     $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a(n)\,(b^2 n)^{-(x + i y)} = b^{-2(x + i y)} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a(n)\,n^{-(x + i y)}$$
     in the domain where the series converge, and on the bare Gaussian profile $\varphi_n(X) = X^{5/2} n^{-3}\exp(-X^2/n^2)$ satisfies the exact dilation identity
     $$\varphi_{b^2 n}(X) = b^{-1}\varphi_n(X / b^2) \qquad (X > 0).$$
   - *Running-index squaring (power lift)*: squaring the summation index produces the square-supported zeta series with doubled complex argument,
     $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (n^2)^{-(x + i y)} = \zeta(2x + 2 i y),$$
     initially as an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series for $x > 1/2$.
   - *Liouville convolution identity*:
     $$\mathbf{1} * \lambda = \mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{square}}, \qquad \zeta(x + i y)\,L_\lambda(x + i y) = \zeta(2x + 2 i y),$$
     initially as an identity of absolutely convergent Dirichlet series for $x > 1$, and then as a meromorphic identity where continued.
   - *Transcendental-grade transport*: $X = \tau^K$ ($K \in \mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{R}}$) parameterizes the canonical logarithmic integration window $[Y, \tau Y]$.

Neither fixed square dilation nor running-index squaring alone proves pole-free continuation of $L_\lambda(x + i y)$ to $x > 1/2$ or the original $\tau$-grade compatibility principle. ### 4.1 Divisor-Core `star` Involution and Exact Support Bijection With Zero Coprimality Assumption (`Theorem 2`)

For any positive integer divisor $e = 2^s c f^2 \ge 1$ ($s \ge 0$, $c$ odd squarefree, $f$ odd — **no coprimality assumption between $c$ and $f$ is made anywhere**), define `squarefreeCoreStar` $a \star c := a c / \gcd(a, c)^2$.
